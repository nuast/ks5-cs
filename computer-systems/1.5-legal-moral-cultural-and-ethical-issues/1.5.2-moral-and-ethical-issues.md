# 1.5.2 Moral and Ethical Issues

> **Component:** 01 — Computer Systems | **Section:** 1.5 | **Sub-section:** 1.5.2

---

## What you need to know

Beyond legality, computing raises significant **moral and ethical** questions about what we *should* do, even when it may be legal. This topic requires you to think critically and discuss multiple perspectives. OCR expects **discussion** and **evaluation** skills here — not just description.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Ethics** | The branch of philosophy concerned with what is morally right and wrong |
| **Digital divide** | The gap between those who have access to digital technology and those who do not |
| **Artificial intelligence (AI)** | Systems that perform tasks typically requiring human intelligence |
| **Algorithmic bias** | When an AI system produces systematically prejudiced results due to biased training data or design |
| **E-waste** | Discarded electronic equipment, often containing toxic materials |
| **Surveillance** | Monitoring of individuals' activities, often by governments or corporations |
| **Censorship** | The suppression or restriction of information |
| **Open access** | Making information freely available online without paywalls |
| **Intellectual property (IP)** | Legal rights protecting creative works |
| **Whistleblowing** | Revealing wrongdoing (e.g. illegal surveillance) to the public |

## Core notes

### Ethical frameworks

When evaluating ethical issues, consider multiple perspectives:

| Framework | Focus |
|-----------|-------|
| **Utilitarian** | What produces the greatest good for the greatest number? |
| **Rights-based** | What rights do individuals have that should not be violated? |
| **Virtue ethics** | What would a person of good character do? |

Exam questions often ask you to **discuss** — give arguments for AND against, then reach a conclusion.

### The digital divide

**Definition:** Inequality in access to computers, internet, and digital literacy.

**Causes:**
- Economic inequality (cost of devices and connectivity)
- Geographic — rural/remote areas with poor infrastructure
- Age — older generations less comfortable with technology
- Disability — accessibility barriers

**Consequences:**
- Educational disadvantage
- Employment disadvantage
- Reduced access to government services, healthcare, banking
- Deepens existing social inequalities

**Closing the divide:**
- Government investment in broadband infrastructure
- Subsidised devices for low-income households
- Digital literacy programmes

### AI and ethics

| Issue | Explanation |
|-------|------------|
| **Bias** | AI trained on biased data reflects and amplifies those biases (e.g. facial recognition less accurate on darker skin tones) |
| **Accountability** | Who is responsible when an AI makes a harmful decision? |
| **Autonomy** | Should AI systems make life-or-death decisions (e.g. autonomous weapons, medical diagnosis)? |
| **Privacy** | AI enables mass surveillance and data profiling |
| **Job displacement** | Automation may replace many jobs |
| **Opacity** | Many AI models are "black boxes" — decisions cannot be easily explained |

### Environmental impact of computing

**Problems:**
- Data centres consume vast amounts of electricity (cooling and computing)
- Manufacturing devices uses rare and toxic materials
- E-waste — disposed devices often go to landfill or are shipped to developing countries
- Cryptocurrency mining is extremely energy-intensive

**Responses:**
- Green data centres using renewable energy (e.g. Google, Microsoft)
- Right-to-repair legislation
- Responsible e-waste recycling schemes
- More energy-efficient chips (reduced power consumption)

### Censorship vs freedom of information

**Arguments for censorship/restrictions:**
- Protecting children from harmful content
- Preventing radicalisation and terrorism
- Protecting national security
- Reducing misinformation/fake news

**Arguments against censorship / for freedom of information:**
- Right to free expression is a fundamental human right
- Censorship can be used by authoritarian governments to suppress dissent
- Difficult to define where to draw the line
- Open access to knowledge drives education and innovation

### Privacy and surveillance

**Tension:** Governments argue surveillance is needed for national security; individuals argue it violates their right to privacy.

**Examples:**
- GCHQ mass surveillance (revealed by Edward Snowden, 2013)
- CCTV networks; facial recognition in public spaces
- Corporate data collection (social media, search engines)
- Employer monitoring of remote workers

### Responsibilities of computing professionals

Computing professionals (developers, IT staff, security researchers) have ethical duties:

- **Do no harm** — consider the potential negative impact of systems
- **Confidentiality** — protect user data
- **Honesty** — report security vulnerabilities responsibly
- **Inclusivity** — consider accessibility and bias
- **Whistleblowing** — obligation to report illegal activity

Codes of conduct are provided by professional bodies (e.g. BCS — British Computer Society).

## Worked examples

### Example — Discussing AI bias

**Question:** Discuss the ethical implications of using AI to make loan approval decisions.

**Arguments for use:**
- Faster and more consistent decisions than humans
- May reduce some forms of human prejudice

**Arguments against:**
- AI trained on historical data may perpetuate historical discrimination
- Applicants cannot easily challenge an opaque algorithmic decision
- Accountability unclear — who is responsible for a wrong decision?

**Conclusion:** AI can assist but should not replace human oversight; systems must be audited for bias; individuals need a right to explanation.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ **Mistake:** Treating this as a factual topic with right/wrong answers.  
  ✅ **Correction:** Ethical questions require **balanced discussion** — present multiple perspectives, acknowledge counter-arguments, and reach a **reasoned conclusion**.

- ❌ **Mistake:** Saying "the digital divide only affects developing countries."  
  ✅ **Correction:** The digital divide exists within wealthy countries too — between urban/rural areas, income groups, age groups, and people with disabilities.

## Exam focus

- **Discuss** — the ethical implications of a scenario (AI use, surveillance, etc.)
- **Evaluate** — the arguments for and against censorship / freedom of information
- **Describe** — what the digital divide is and its consequences
- **Explain** — ethical responsibilities of computing professionals

> 💡 This topic commonly appears as **extended response questions** (6–9 marks). Show you can argue both sides. Use the word "however" to introduce counterarguments. Always reach a conclusion.
