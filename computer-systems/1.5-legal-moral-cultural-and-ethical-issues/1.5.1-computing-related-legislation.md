# 1.5.1 Computing Related Legislation

> **Component:** 01 — Computer Systems | **Section:** 1.5 | **Sub-section:** 1.5.1

## What you need to know

Computers and the internet create legal challenges around privacy, intellectual property, crime, and access to information. You need to know the key pieces of UK (and relevant international) legislation that govern the use of computing, and be able to apply them to real-world scenarios.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Data Protection Act 2018** | UK law implementing GDPR; governs how personal data is collected, stored, and used |
| **UK GDPR** | UK General Data Protection Regulation — the core data protection framework post-Brexit |
| **Personal data** | Any information that can identify a living individual |
| **Data controller** | An organisation or individual that determines how personal data is processed |
| **Data processor** | An organisation that processes data on behalf of a data controller |
| **Computer Misuse Act 1990** | UK law making unauthorised access to computer systems a criminal offence |
| **Copyright** | Legal protection for original creative works (software, music, text, images) |
| **Intellectual property** | Legal rights protecting creations of the mind |
| **RIPA** | Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000 — governs how public bodies may conduct surveillance |
| **Freedom of Information Act** | Gives individuals the right to request information held by public authorities |

## Core notes

### Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR

The **UK GDPR** (implemented by the DPA 2018) requires that personal data be:

1. **Processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently**
2. **Collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes**
3. **Adequate, relevant, and limited** to what is necessary
4. **Accurate** and kept up to date
5. **Stored no longer than necessary**
6. **Processed securely** (appropriate technical/organisational measures)
7. **Held responsibly** — the data controller is accountable

**Individual rights under UK GDPR:**
- Right to access their own data
- Right to rectification (correct inaccurate data)
- Right to erasure ("right to be forgotten")
- Right to restrict processing
- Right to data portability
- Right to object to processing

### Computer Misuse Act 1990

Three offences (in increasing severity):

| Section | Offence | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **s1** | Unauthorised access to computer material | Logging into someone else's account without permission |
| **s2** | Unauthorised access with intent to commit further offences | Hacking a bank server to transfer money |
| **s3** | Unauthorised acts with intent to impair a computer | Deploying malware/ransomware; DDoS attacks |

### Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988

- Software is protected by **copyright** automatically on creation
- Illegal to copy, distribute, or modify software without authorisation
- **Open source licences** (e.g. GPL, MIT) grant specific rights
- **Creative Commons** licences for other creative works
- Illegal to download/share music, films, books without permission

### RIPA 2000

- Allows government agencies to lawfully intercept communications and conduct surveillance
- Requires authorisation; must be necessary and proportionate
- Relevant in discussions of privacy vs national security

### Freedom of Information Act 2000

- Gives any person the right to request information held by **public authorities** (government, councils, NHS, schools, police)
- The authority must respond within 20 working days
- Exemptions exist (e.g. national security, personal data, commercial confidentiality)

## Worked examples

### Example — Applying the Computer Misuse Act

**Scenario:** A disgruntled former employee uses their old login credentials (that the company forgot to revoke) to access customer records.

- **s1 offence:** Unauthorised access — their employment ended, so the access is now unauthorised.
- If they copy or sell the data: **s3** or additional offences under DPA 2018.

### Example — Applying the DPA 2018

**Scenario:** An online shop collects email addresses for order confirmations, but also adds customers to a marketing list without asking.

- Violates principle 2 (data collected for specified purposes)
- Violates principle 1 (not processed lawfully — no consent for marketing)

## Common mistakes

- ❌ **Mistake:** Saying the Computer Misuse Act only applies to hacking external systems.  
  ✅ **Correction:** Even accessing a system you have some authorisation for, beyond your permitted scope, can be an offence.

- ❌ **Mistake:** Saying copyright only applies to software you register.  
  ✅ **Correction:** Copyright is **automatic** — it exists from the moment an original work is created.

## Exam focus

- **Identify** — which Act applies to a described scenario
- **Describe** — the principles of the DPA 2018
- **State** — the three offences under the Computer Misuse Act
- **Apply** — legislation to a given situation and explain which law is broken and why

> 💡 Scenario application questions are very common — "A company does X. Identify which law is broken and explain why." Always name the act AND explain how the scenario breaches it.

## Quick-check questions

1. Name three principles of the Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR.
2. What are the three offences under the Computer Misuse Act?
3. Which act protects software from being illegally copied?
4. What does RIPA allow?
5. What right does the Freedom of Information Act give individuals?

<details>
<summary>Outline answers</summary>

1. Any three: lawfully/fairly/transparently; specified purpose; adequate/relevant/limited; accurate; not kept longer than necessary; secure; accountable.
2. s1: Unauthorised access. s2: Unauthorised access with intent to commit further offences. s3: Unauthorised acts intending to impair a computer.
3. Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988.
4. Allows government agencies to lawfully conduct surveillance and intercept communications.
5. The right to request information held by public authorities.

</details>
